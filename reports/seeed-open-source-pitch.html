Interlock · built at HackAIWoo · for Seeed Studio

Interlock lets people and AI models control the SO‑ARM101 safely. We want to give it to the community.

Interlock is open-source software that sits between the SO‑ARM101 and every program that drives it. It checks each move before the arm moves. It shows a person what the arm is doing. It lets any AI model control the arm through one standard interface (MCP). We built it at HackAIWoo and we propose to share it with all SO‑ARM101 owners.

Interlock LinkThe connection for AI models. It uses MCP, a standard way for AI models to use tools.
Interlock ConsoleA web page for people: live camera, 3D model, controls and STOP.
refusedCore said no. The arm did not move. The reply says why.
frozenThe arm stopped during a move and holds still, because it touched something or a person took over.
The problem

New SO‑ARM101 owners meet four problems that the hardware cannot solve

First, each script opens the serial port by itself, so two programs cannot run together ("Port is in use"). Second, there is no live view of servo load or temperature. Third, an AI model can send any command, and nothing checks it before the motors move. Fourth, runs are not recorded, so nobody can learn from them. Every owner solves these alone today. We solved them once, in the open.

What we built

Five parts. Four work today. The temperature check is next.

Each card shows what works today and what we would share.

1 · Interlock Core working

One program owns the arm, with one documented API for all others

Today

  • Only Core opens the serial port. When it starts or stops, it leaves the motors as they were, so the arm does not drop.
  • It reads every servo 20 times a second: position, target, load, motor power and temperature. Other programs get this over HTTP and WebSocket.
  • It also runs the camera feed, object detection, run recording and saved poses. It only accepts connections from the same computer.
  • It works with LeRobot. Core can lend the arm to lerobot-teleoperate and take it back.
  • One file, API.md, describes every request.
  • 167 tests run on a simulated arm, so people can help without owning one.

We would share

  • The design and the API, as a reference for the SO‑ARM100 repository.
  • The simulated arm for testing.
  • The LeRobot hand-over, which we already use.
2 · Interlock Link (MCP) working

Any AI model can control the arm, and every answer it gets is clear

Today

  • 31 tools, such as look, pose, align_to, grasp, release and stop, plus search and replay of recorded runs.
  • Every move returns one of three results: ok, frozen or refused, with a short reason in plain words.
  • Grabbing follows fixed steps. After three tries to close the gripper, the AI must stop and ask a person.
  • We tested the tools with a small AI model (Haiku) on purpose. Its mistakes showed us what to fix in the tools.

We would share

  • Interlock Link, as the first standard AI interface for the SO‑ARM101.
  • The driving-the-arm guide: when the AI should look, grab, stop or ask.
  • The list of results and reasons, which other low-cost arms can reuse.
3 · Interlock Console working

A person can watch the arm, take control, and press STOP

Today

  • A 3D model of the arm, built from the official SO‑ARM101 parts and moved by the same maths as Core.
  • The live gripper camera with a 1 cm grid on the table. Detected objects show their size and position in cm.
  • Angle, load and temperature for each joint. Sliders, small steps, saved poses, a STOP button and a control switch.
  • A log of every move result, from people and from AI models.

We would share

  • Interlock Console: one folder, it opens in a browser, nothing to build.
  • The 3D viewer, which any SO‑ARM101 project can use.
4 · Servo temperature logging workscheck is next

The Console once showed 71 °C. The real value was 36 °C. We found the cause and fixed it.

Today

  • We studied about 14 hours of use on 22 to 23 October: 1,250 AI tool calls, 340 moves and 17 recorded runs, for all six STS3215 servos.
  • The Console showed the elbow servo at 71 °C, above the 70 °C limit set in every servo. The value was old: Core did not update temperature while the leader arm had control. A new reading showed 36 °C.
  • The fix runs since 08:36 on 23 October. Temperature now updates every 2.5 seconds, and each servo's temperature, load and position is logged every 10 seconds.
  • In the first 2 hours of the log (427 records), the shoulder servo was the warmest, at 38.1 °C on average. It holds the arm up, so it carries about four times the load of the others. The highest real reading was 44 °C, 26 °C below the limit. We think holding a load causes most of the heat, but we have not proven it.
  • The log also caught two false readings on the wrist servo: 74 °C and 62 °C, each for one 10-second sample, with 36 °C before and after. A servo cannot heat and cool that fast. One in four records had no temperature at all.
  • In the Console, each servo's colour shows its temperature. A joint is marked hot at 55 °C.

Next

  • Keep logging, and compare heat for holding, carrying and long reaches.
  • Add a temperature check next to the load check, so a hot servo slows down or rests before it shuts itself off. The check must ignore single false readings.
  • Write a guide for safe servo use, based on Seeed's data (see our request).
5 · Safety rules working

A person decides when the AI gets control. Anyone can stop it. Only a person can restart it.

Today

  • A person starts in control. Only a person can give control to an AI. The AI cannot take it.
  • STOP works for anyone. Only a person can restart after STOP. The old STOP file also still works.
  • Load and lag check on every move, always on. If it trips, the arm stops where it is and holds still.
  • Table check before every move. A move that comes within 1.5 cm of the table is refused, and the reply gives the distance in cm.
  • Joint limits: values past a limit are cut to the limit, and the reply says so. Only one move runs at a time. A second move gets busy.
  • Turning the motors off while the arm is raised is refused unless a person confirms it.
  • Tested on purpose: the AI ran 28 rounds alone. Each round ended with one bad command, of 9 kinds: into the table, below it, too far, behind the base, or a servo name that does not exist. All 28 were refused. The arm did not move.

We would share

  • The checks and control rules, written as a safety guide for low-cost arms controlled by AI.
  • The tests that prove each check, including that the AI cannot turn them off.
Test results

Interlock stopped 43 of 1,013 logged moves, each with a clear reason

We do not report a success rate, because many runs that day were not recorded. We report what the log did record. In 8.3 hours, Core logged 1,013 moves and refused 43 of them before the arm moved. 19 of those paths went into the table or within 1.5 cm of it; the worst went 5.0 cm into it. 18 came while someone else had control. Two AI moves were frozen when a person took over with the leader arm. In test round 4, the camera tracker followed the white gripper finger by mistake. After 12 moves the tool answered not_aligned and did not close the gripper.

Our request

We ask Seeed for three things

  1. A home. A repository under Seeed, or a link in the SO‑ARM101 docs, so new owners can find Interlock.
  2. Servo data. Which STS3215 datasheet fits the 12 V arm, what happens at 70 °C, and whether a steady 10 to 20% holding load is safe over time. Our temperature check should use your numbers, not our guesses.
  3. A review. An engineer to check our safety rules before we publish them as a guide.

In return: an open-source licence (MIT style), tests that run without hardware, and a maintainer for the first six months.

Sources and limits

Where each number comes from, and what we have not proven

  • Code numbers come from the main branch at 078c7be: 167 tests in arm-daemon/tests (Interlock Core's code still lives in the arm-daemon/ folder), 31 MCP tools, and 52 recorded runs.
  • Move and refusal counts come from arm-daemon/logs/20261023.jsonl (1,013 records, 8.3 hours). They show what the log recorded. Not every run that day was logged, so we give no success rate.
  • The 28-round test is in reports/guardrail-loop/ on branch claude/arm-guardrail-test-loop-bb691d (23 Oct, 08:44 to 09:27). "Too far" and "unknown servo" refusals come from Link, so they are not in Core's log.
  • The 71 °C finding comes from reports/servo-health/servo-health-report.html (branch claude/servo-health-report-2f4a19). The fix (3de86c7) is on the main branch and running. The 2-hour numbers come from arm-daemon/logs/health/20261023.jsonl, 08:36 to 10:38. Maximum values leave out the two false readings.
  • The 70 °C limit comes from a backup of our servos' settings (address 13). We could only find the 7.4 V datasheet; our arm runs at about 12 V.
  • Interlock does not detect hands or people yet. The checks work on servo data only: load, lag, table, limits, STOP and who has control.
  • The table check depends on a model of the arm. That model is wrong when the arm rests on an object.
  • The licence and the six-month maintainer are offers. The team has not decided them yet.
  • Object detection (YOLO-World) misses small objects seen from above 6 cm. When that happens, the AI points at the object's position in the camera image instead.
