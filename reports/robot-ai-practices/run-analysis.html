Every miss closed the jaws beside the object. Every success closed them with the whole object centred between the tips.

Twelve episodes were reprocessed from their recordings: six human teleop runs and six agent runs. Seven succeeded, four failed outright, one is a thin-object case the data cannot settle. The human runs show the pattern that works: survey from above with the whole object in frame, settle the lateral alignment, descend slowly, close once. The agent misses share one mechanic: the jaws closed while the object was off to one side or half under a finger. The skills layer now refuses to do that.

What the successes have in common

Full object view before any descent

Every human pick opened the jaws between 4.8 and 6.7 cm with the whole carton inside the frame, then descended. The agent's one clean pick (04:17) also corrected from a view that contained the whole carton. Both late agent successes came right after a survey from 11 cm.

Lateral settling, then a paced descent

Human last-5-seconds lateral motion before a successful close: 2.5 to 4.5 cm, descent 0.1 to 1.2 cm/s. Agent misses: 4 to 12.5 cm of lateral motion and a close straight after the last move, without re-checking.

Repeatable grasp numbers

Human carton grasps closed at 3.5 to 3.7 cm tool height with jaws stopping at 7.3 %. The agent's holds closed at 0.6 to 1.1 cm and stopped at 3.5 to 5.9 %: edge pinches that carried the carton dangling. The card's grasp_tool_z_cm now tells the next run where the human closed.

Regrip instead of forcing

Three human runs opened and re-approached after a bad first close. No human run ever closed twice at the same spot.

What the misses have in common

Closing beside the object

Seven of the nine agent empty closes have the carton visible at the side of the frame or half under the fixed jaw in the keyframe (04:57 attempts 2 to 5, 05:31 attempts 1 and 2, 03:51). The jaws were commanded to close without any check that the object sat between the tips.

Half-identified object

04:57's first four attempts planned from close-up views where the carton's size, orientation and the rolled image axes were guessed. The fork's own note: "survey the whole object first".

Mismeasurement

The pixel-to-table projection was 5 cm off in both fork runs. 03:51 trusted the kinematic height and found the carton 2 cm higher than modelled.

Wrong verdicts

The jaw percent reads 0.9 to 1.1 % for both a miss (03:51) and a real hold on a thin edge (04:17 settled 4.6 % but the recording's later value was 1.5 %). The old cards called two successes failures and the skills' 3 % rule would have too.

Not changed, and why

  • descend_to_contact threshold. Needs real-arm data at low speed with a light object; the fork measured that 350 load units never fired on a carton. A lag-based trigger is the plan; untested on hardware, so not shipped tonight.
  • Projection bias. The gain measurement compensates per run. A proper two-height calibration of the fingertip offset needs the arm and a marker.
  • Thin objects. The phone case stays unclear in the cards. Label it on demo_stop.
  • Running session. The session driving the arm now keeps the old skills until its MCP reconnects. My heads-up to it was held by its permission mode and expired, so it has not been told.

Method

Attempts were extracted as transitions of the gripper from above 15 % open to 15 % or below, with the settle value over the next 2 s, the lift while the jaws stayed closed in the following 6 s, the descent speed over the preceding 3 s and the lateral path over the preceding 5 s. Keyframes nearest each close were inspected. Agent notes come from meta.json as written by the sessions that drove. Leader runs have no notes; their labels are from the data and the frames.
