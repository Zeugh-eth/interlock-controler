01 · Name

Why “Interlock”

An interlock is the machine-safety device that stops a machine unless conditions are safe: the door switch on a cell, the key that only one person can hold. That is exactly what the kit does. The daemon is the single owner of the serial bus, the holder switch decides who may move the arm (human, agent, leader, or nobody), and every command passes guards that clamp, freeze or refuse.

It is a real engineering word, short, easy to say, and it reads as a promise: nothing moves unless it is allowed to.

02 · Logo

A padlock built from a robot arm

The shackle is two arm links meeting at two round joints; the body is the guarded envelope; the lamp is live state. Read fast it is a lock. Read slow it is an arm. Closed, symmetric and sitting on a wide base, it never looks like it is about to move.

Construction

  • 64-unit grid, 4-unit module. Body 40 × 28 with an 8-unit radius; the same radius the UI uses for buttons and cards.
  • Shackle stroke 6, joints r5 with a 3 stroke; lamp r5 on the body's centre line.
  • Clear space on every side: one quarter of the mark's width (the dashed square).
  • Minimum size: mark 16 px (use the favicon cut), lockup 96 px wide.
  • The lamp is the only colour in the mark. Clear means the arm is in a safe, known state; in live UI it may switch to Caution or Stop to show the real state, nothing else.
Don't rotate it. It should never look in motion.
Don't paint the lamp red as decoration. Red is STOP.
Don't stretch or squash.
Don't place on gradients or busy photos.

The colour theory behind it

  • Complement for STOP. Clear (hue ≈ 162°) sits almost exactly opposite Stop (≈ 7°). Every screen is mostly green-tinted neutrals and a little Clear, so the one red button gets maximum contrast against everything around it.
  • Safety meanings are borrowed, not invented. Green = safe, amber = caution, red = stop follows ISO 3864 and every factory floor. Operators read them without a legend.
  • Holders live in the cool half of the wheel (cobalt, violet), away from the safety hues, so "who has the arm" is never confused with "is it safe".
  • Low chroma, high value. Neutrals stay above 90 % lightness and under 3 % saturation: light, clean, quiet. Saturation is spent only where a decision is needed.
  • Contrast first. Every text pairing meets WCAG AA (≥ 4.5:1); ratios are printed on each chip. Amber is never text on light ground; use Caution text #9A6A00.
04 · Typography

IBM Plex Sans and IBM Plex Mono

Plex was drawn for engineering: straight-sided, slightly mechanical, very legible at small sizes, and the Sans and Mono share one skeleton, so labels and numbers sit together without friction. Both are free (SIL OFL). Sans for words people read; Mono for anything the machine says: joint values, ports, outcomes, tool names, timestamps.

05 · Shape

Squares hold, circles turn, triangles warn

Shape carries meaning the same way colour does. Interlock's vocabulary is small and literal.

Rounded rectangle

Structure, stability, the guarded envelope. Panels, cards, buttons, the lock body. Sits flat on its longest side.

Circle

Joints, motion, live state. Status dots, joint markers in the 3D view, the lamp. A circle always means "this can move or change".

Orthogonal link

Straight segments at 90°, rounded joins. Connectors, dividers, diagrams. Pragmatic, no flourish, no diagonals.

Triangle

Reserved for warnings, always with rounded corners and Caution fill. Never decorative, never in the logo.

Radius 4 · 8 · 14

Chips and inputs 4, buttons and cards 8, panels and icons 14. Nothing has a sharp corner, like the arm: no edge that can catch.

4-px grid

Spacing steps 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48. Same module as the logo grid.
1 px Rule lines, 2 px focus rings in Clear. No drop shadows; depth comes from Fog vs Paper.

Flat, hairline

A light, flat surface keeps the camera feed and 3D arm as the brightest, most detailed things on screen.

Voice

  • Say what happened, then what to do. "Froze on a load spike at the elbow. Look before retrying." Not "Oops, something went wrong."
  • Refused is calm. A refusal means the guard worked and nothing moved. Grey, not red.
  • Units always. Degrees, percent open, centimetres. Mono, tabular numbers.
  • Name the holder. Every message that moves the arm says who moved it.
07 · Rules

Five rules that hold the brand together

Do keep Red for STOP and nothing else, in product, slides and stickers.
Don't use red for errors, deletes or "important". Refused is grey, frozen is amber.
Do let the camera feed and 3D arm be the most colourful thing on screen.
Don't add gradients, glows or drop shadows. Depth is Fog under Paper.
Do put machine words in Mono: shoulder_lift, :8800, held.
Don't draw sharp corners or diagonals in UI chrome; radius 4 · 8 · 14 only.
Do show the holder colour wherever the arm can be commanded.
Don't animate the logo or make it look in motion. The lock is still.
