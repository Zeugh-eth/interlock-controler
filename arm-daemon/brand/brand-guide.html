01 · Name

Why “Interlock”

In machine safety, an interlock is what lets a machine run only when conditions are right. That is the kit's job. The daemon is the single owner of the serial bus, the holder switch decides who may move the arm (human, agent, leader, or nobody), and every command passes guards that clamp, freeze or refuse.

The word also means linked together: arm links, joints, and people and agents working with each other. The brand leans on that second meaning. The logo shows an arm working inside a safe halo, not a lock.

02 · Logo

An arm at work inside its halo

A three-link arm (base, upper arm, forearm) drawn with fully rounded ends and white joints, reaching for a light-blue point: the thing it has been trusted with. Around it sits the halo, the safe workspace the guards keep. Nothing in it is pointed, and the whole mark sits inside a circle, the softest closed shape there is.

Construction

  • 64-unit grid, 4-unit module. Halo r30 centred; arm stroke 7 with round caps and joins, so every end is a half-circle.
  • Joints are white r2.6 dots, the held point is a Sky r3.4 dot. Base is a 22 × 7 pill.
  • The arm is optically centred in the halo (shifted +4, −3), with the reach to the right: forward, like reading.
  • Clear space: one quarter of the mark's width on every side. Minimum: 16 px (favicon cut), lockup 96 px wide.
  • Only two blues and white inside the mark. It never takes a safety colour.
Don't mirror it. The arm reaches forward, to the right.
Don't recolour in safety colours. Red is STOP.
Don't stretch or squash.
Don't drop the halo. The arm always works inside it.
03 · Colour

Whites, soft grays, one light blue

Interlock is airy and quiet: mostly white, with cool soft grays for structure and a single light blue that means "this is Interlock, and it's fine". Every gray carries a trace of the same blue hue (≈ 212°), so the whole surface reads as one material. Warm colours appear only when the arm needs attention.

Human = Ink, released = Pebble. "ok" is Sky Deep, refused is Cloud with Slate text: a refusal means a guard worked, so it stays calm and gray.

The colour theory behind it

  • Analogous and cool for calm. Every neutral and both blues sit around 206–213°. One hue family with low saturation reads as clean, trustworthy and quiet, the way a clean lab or a hospital reads.
  • Complements for alerts. Caution (≈ 39°) and Stop (≈ 8°) sit across the wheel from the blue. On an all-cool screen the warm states are the only warm things, so they are seen instantly without being loud.
  • High value, low chroma. The surface stays above 90 % lightness. Sky is light enough to sit on white without shouting; Sky Deep carries text where contrast is needed.
  • Holders stay in the cool half (Iris, Lilac), next to the brand blue but distinct from it, so "who has the arm" never gets confused with "is it safe".
  • Safety meanings are borrowed, not invented. Amber = caution and red = stop follow ISO 3864, so operators read them without a legend. Every text pairing meets WCAG AA (≥ 4.5:1).
04 · Typography

DM Sans and DM Mono

DM Sans is a low-contrast geometric sans with round bowls and open shapes: friendly, modern and very clean, without being cute. DM Mono is drawn on the same skeleton, so labels and numbers sit beside words without a jolt. Both are free (SIL OFL). Sans for words people read; Mono for anything the machine says: joint values, ports, outcomes, tool names, timestamps.

05 · Shape

Round ends, soft corners, nothing that can catch

Shape theory: circles and rounded forms read as safe, gentle and approachable; straight runs read as dependable and practical. Interlock uses both, in fixed roles, and drops the sharp corner entirely.

Circle · safety

The halo, status dots, joints. A circle means a protected space or a live state.

Capsule · motion

Straight runs with round ends: arm links, pills, progress and load bars. Practical, but soft at every end.

Soft rectangle · order

Cards and panels. Stable, flat on the ground, generous radius. The pragmatic shape.

Triangle · warning only

Deeply rounded, always in Caution. The one pointed idea in the system, kept for when attention is needed.

Radius 6 · 12 · 20 · pill

Inputs 6, buttons and cards 12, panels and icons 20, toggles and statuses as pills.

Voice

  • Say what happened, then what to do. "Paused on a load spike at the elbow. Look before retrying." Not "Oops, something went wrong."
  • Refused is calm. A refusal means the guard worked and nothing moved. Gray, not red.
  • Units always. Degrees, percent open, centimetres. Mono, tabular numbers.
  • Name the holder. Every message that moves the arm says who moved it.
07 · Rules

Eight rules that hold the brand together

Do keep surfaces mostly white and Frost; let soft grays carry structure.
Don't use red for errors, deletes or "important". Red is STOP; refused is gray, frozen is amber.
Do use Sky for highlights and fills, Sky Deep for text and buttons.
Don't use pure black, heavy shadows, gradients or glows.
Do put machine words in Mono: shoulder_lift, :8800, held.
Don't draw sharp corners. Radius 6 · 12 · 20 · pill only.
Do show the holder colour wherever the arm can be commanded.
Don't put the arm outside its halo or recolour the mark.
